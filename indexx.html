01 / About

Language quality is more than correctness.

It is about meaning, context, register, cultural texture, and how language actually works inside a culture.

My work sits at the intersection of Brazilian Portuguese linguistics, software localization, and AI language quality.

My experience includes English-to-Brazilian Portuguese localization, machine translation post-editing, light post-editing, linguistic QA, audiovisual translation, and linguistic data workflows.

In software-related projects, I refine AI-generated interface content and assess language against the intended function, product context, terminology, and Brazilian Portuguese conventions. In separate quality-assessment work, I have also documented why AI-generated output was incorrect and assessed the severity of the issue.

I evaluate Brazilian Portuguese not as a generic "Portuguese" variant, but as its own system — with its own rhythm, its own humor, its own regional texture, and its own evolving slang — and I judge AI outputs against that specificity, not against a neutral standard. My evaluation work also includes pairwise comparison of AI-generated responses, structured preference criteria, and Chain-of-Thought audits of model reasoning.

Case 05 — Pairwise Response Evaluation & CoT Audit

Pairwise Response Evaluation & CoT Audit

Two fluent Brazilian Portuguese responses to the same prompt — ranked against structured preference criteria, with a Chain-of-Thought audit of each answer and a documented rationale in English.

Prompt · PT-BR

"Um amigo te chamou pra sair amanhã, mas você está sem grana. Como você recusa sem soar grosso?"
Response APreferred

"Pô, amanhã não vai dar pra mim. Tô duro esse mês, mas semana que vem a gente marca alguma coisa — pode ser?"

Natural · regional · preserves friendship · offers alternative
Response BRejected

"Infelizmente não poderei comparecer. Estou sem recursos financeiros no momento. Fica para a próxima."

Grammatically correct · register mismatch · loses the invitation

Structured preference criteria

Criterion A B
Linguistic accuracy✓ 4✓ 4
Natural fluency & idiomaticity✓ 4✗ 2
Cultural authenticity (register, tone)✓ 4✗ 1
Instruction adherence✓ 4✗ 2
Aggregate16 / 169 / 16

Chain-of-Thought audit

A · reasoning trace
  1. Friend invited me out.
  2. I have no money this month.
  3. I need to refuse politely.
  4. Use colloquial register — matches friend context.
  5. Offer an alternative to preserve the relationship.
✓ Conclusion follows from premises.
B · reasoning trace
  1. Friend invited me out.
  2. I should refuse.
  3. Use formal language to be polite.
⚠ "Formal" ≠ "polite" in friendship context. Post-hoc reasoning.
Edge case flagged · severity: major

Response B is grammatically flawless but pragmatically broken — formal register for a close-friend context reads as cold or sarcastic in Brazilian Portuguese. Flagged as a cultural authenticity failure, not a grammar error.

Written rationale (EN) · final ranking

Preferred: Response A. Both are grammatically correct, but only A preserves the pragmatic contract of the prompt — refusing a friend without sounding rude. A uses colloquial register ("pô", "tô duro") that matches the friendship context and offers an alternative. B is formally correct but pragmatically inadequate; its reasoning trace is post-hoc. Edge case flagged; severity: major.

05 / Work process

Intervene at the right level.

Good evaluation does not mean rewriting everything. It means applying the right quality criterion to the actual problem.

01

Understand context

Identify audience, product function, language variant, references, and terminology.

02

Assess output

Compare meaning, fluency, naturalness, terminology, and contextual suitability.

03

Classify issues

Separate meaning errors, terminology violations, grammar, register mismatches, and stylistic preferences.

04

Make the edit

Use targeted changes or deeper rewriting according to severity and purpose.

05

Explain decisions

Provide actionable feedback that supports consistency and future quality.
